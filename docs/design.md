## Problem Statement

Carbon-aware scheduling tools tell every flexible electricity user (EV fleets, data centers, office buildings) to shift load into the same "clean" hours. When many large loads follow the same signal, there is not enough spare clean generation, so fossil plants ramp up to serve the rush. Realized savings fall short of what the signal promised, and emissions can rise. Martin, Powell & Rajagopal (Nature Communications, 2025; verify volume/article number before it goes on a slide) showed this for EV charging on a Western US grid model and proposed "Cascading": one aggregator coordinates its EVs in smaller groups.

Open gap: coordination across *independent, competing* companies with different load types, and a way to pay each one for the emissions its participation in coordination actually avoided, beyond what it would have saved acting alone.

## What Makes This Cool

- One chart tells the story: total CO2 versus flexible load (% of regional peak), for four strategies. It shows the herding gap (sum of solo savings vs realized joint savings) and how much coordination recovers.
- It turns coordination into money: every participant gets an "avoided-carbon receipt" (bid, assigned hours, emissions vs the go-it-alone counterfactual, Shapley share, payout at a stated carbon price), settled on actual grid conditions.
- Framing: a carbon clearinghouse, not another scheduler. The hard part of paying for avoided emissions is the counterfactual; the calibrated simulator answers it, and the baseline is chosen so companies are paid only for what coordination adds.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The novel claim is coordination across competing companies plus payouts for avoided emissions. The herding insight is prior work; cite the Nature Communications paper.
2. Herding must show up in our own simulator before anything else gets built.
3. (Revised after second opinion) ML forecasts the future grid; a calibrated simulator answers the what-ifs. The forecast model predicts next-24h demand, wind and solar from real EIA-930 history. Carbon intensity is never an ML label taken from the simulator: the "average intensity" signal is computed from EIA-930 fuel mix x fuel emission factors (for training/eval) and, at plan time, by running the simulator on forecast inputs. No ML model is trained on simulator output.
4. Chalk serves forecasts and features on the re-plan path. (Access is settled in hour 0, with a cutoff at hour 6; see Next Steps.)
5. Payouts use a visible, stated carbon price ($/ton) and a stated pool size.

Dropped: federated learning. The coordinator needs only flexibility bids. Privacy = data minimization: companies submit a bid, never usage history; in a real deployment each company sees only its own slot and receipt. The demo shows the operator/auditor view of all receipts, labeled as such.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Merit-order clearinghouse (CHOSEN)
One real region; merit-order simulator of real plants; Chalk-served forecasts; greedy coordinator; exact Shapley for 3-5 bidders; receipts. Effort M, risk low. Main gap: no transmission congestion.

### Approach B: PyPSA network dispatch
Same clearinghouse with transmission limits and co-optimized dispatch. Most rigorous; effort L-XL, risk med-high for 24h. Not chosen.

### Approach C: Live flexibility exchange (STRETCH, on A's engine)
Audience submits bids from phones; live re-clearing, chart, receipts. Monte Carlo Shapley if more than ~8 bidders. Keep a recorded fallback. Only if A's success criteria are met by ~hour 18.

## Recommended Approach

A, with C as stretch.

### 1. Region data + simulator

- Region: ERCOT recommended (largely islanded, so imports are small). CAISO is the alternative (stronger solar story, large imports).
- Inputs: EIA-930 hourly demand and generation by fuel; EPA eGRID plant capacity, heat rate, CO2 rate; EIA fuel prices. EPA CAMPD hourly unit data is optional extra validation.
- Dispatch rule per hour: residual demand = demand − (nuclear + hydro + wind + solar + net imports from EIA-930). Fill residual demand from the fossil stack sorted by marginal cost (heat rate x fuel price), with each plant's available capacity = nameplate x per-fuel availability factor. Vectorized via cumulative capacity + `searchsorted`.
- Curtailment: EIA-930 wind and solar are post-curtailment, so EIA-930 alone cannot reveal spare clean energy. In hour 1, try to get ERCOT curtailment or available wind/solar capacity data; if found, added load in a curtailed hour is first served by the curtailed MWh at 0 t/MWh. If not found, state plainly that clean-hour absorption is not modeled and that savings estimates are conservative.
- Fitted parameters (named, small): per-fuel availability factor, monthly fuel price scaling. Fit on training days only.
- Validation on held-out days, all reported:
  (a) hourly coal vs gas generation split vs EIA-930 actuals;
  (b) marginal emission factor: regress ΔCO2 on Δfossil generation hour-to-hour in the data (EIA-930 fuel mix x emission factors, or CAMPD) and compare with the simulator's marginal rates;
  (c) hourly fossil CO2 error.
- Output: `emissions(load_profile) -> tons CO2` and per-hour marginal resource (for explanation in the UI).

### 2. Forecast model (ML), served by Chalk

- Gradient-boosted model forecasting next-24h demand, wind and solar from EIA-930 history (features: lags, hour, day of week, rolling averages).
- Chalk surface (trimmed for 24h): point-in-time offline queries for leakage-free training data, windowed aggregation features, model serving called on every re-plan with latency displayed. Bid features served as online features. A live scheduled EIA-930 pull is only added for stretch C (replay uses historical data).
- Fallback: the same `get_forecast(region, as_of)` and `get_bid_features(bid_id)` function signatures backed by a local implementation, so downstream work is never blocked on Chalk.

### 3. Bids and strategies

- Bid model (all load types): interruptible energy E (MWh) to deliver within [earliest, deadline] at up to P_max (MW), on a single 24h horizon (midnight to midnight; no windows crossing midnight). Office HVAC pre-cooling and data-center batch work are approximated this way; stated on a slide.
- Bids are additional load on top of EIA-930 historical demand, under the same rule for every strategy.
- Bidder sizing: stated up front as % of regional peak demand (ERCOT peaks in the tens of GW, so a 100 MW fleet barely moves the margin). The named demo bidders' sizes are fixed before the herding gate from real-world references (published data-center and EV-depot sizes), not tuned to the result. The adoption chart covers all shares anyway.
- Planning vs scoring: every strategy plans from the same forecast inputs (forecast demand/wind/solar run through the simulator). Every strategy is scored by running the simulator on the actual day's EIA-930 inputs.
- Strategies:
  1. Default: each bid runs at P_max from its earliest start until E is met.
  2. Independent-average: each company fills the feasible hours with the lowest forecast average carbon intensity at P_max until E is met (ties: earliest hour), unaware of others. (The standard carbon-aware tool.)
  3. Independent-marginal: each company, alone, runs the same greedy as strategy 4 on only its own blocks, assuming everyone else runs default. Then all execute together. (Isolates the value of coordination from the value of a better signal.)
  4. Coordinated: blocks of 1% of each bid's energy, processed round-robin across bids in order of earliest deadline; each block goes to the feasible hour with the lowest incremental simulated emissions given all blocks already placed; ties broken by earliest hour. Emissions vs load is non-convex, so greedy is not guaranteed optimal; stated. Fallback check at planning time only, on the forecast: if greedy is worse than strategy 3 for a coalition on the forecast, use strategy 3's schedule. This guarantees 4 ≤ 3 only on the forecast, never on actuals; report how often the fallback fires.
- Herding metric (same model throughout): predicted savings = sum over companies of each company's solo savings vs default (that company shifts via strategy 3, everyone else at default). Realized savings = savings when all companies run strategy 3 together. The gap is the herding effect. The same comparison is shown for strategy 2.
- Comparisons on the chart: 2 vs 3 shows the value of a better signal; 3 vs 4 shows the value of coordination (the novel claim).

### 4. Adoption chart

- X-axis: flexible load as % of regional peak demand, using a population of many scaled synthetic bids (randomized windows and sizes from stated distributions, fixed seed, averaged over K draws with a variability band) to get a smooth curve.
- Y-axis: total CO2 for the day, for strategies 1-4, scored on actuals. The chart uses a single day-ahead plan (no rolling re-plans).
- Separately, for the 5 named bidders: average v(S) over all subsets of each size (computed from the same 32 coalition runs used for Shapley).

### 5. Settlement

- Baseline for payment (additionality): everyone acts alone with strategy 3 (independent-marginal). Companies are paid only for what coordination adds beyond what they could save alone. Also show savings vs the default baseline, labeled separately.
- Coalition value v(S) = emissions(all run strategy 3) − emissions(members of S coordinated via strategy 4, non-members run strategy 3), scored on actuals. Strategy 3 is used for non-members as a conservative baseline: it is the best a company could do alone, which keeps payouts lower. Savings against the strategy-2 baseline (the standard carbon-aware tool) are also shown, labeled separately.
- Information: when planning for coalition S, the coordinator knows non-members' strategy-3 schedules and treats them as fixed. So v({i}) can be positive: it is labeled as value from reacting to others' known schedules, not coordination.
- Exact Shapley over all 2^n coalitions (n ≤ 5, i.e. 32 coalition runs), once at end-of-day settlement. Each coalition run replays the full rolling re-plan sequence (32 x 4-6 runs), so every v(S) is computed the same way as the executed schedule.
- Schedules are fixed from the plan; settlement is computed after the fact on actual grid conditions.
- Payout = Shapley tons x carbon price. If total payouts exceed the pool, prorate. A negative share means $0 paid, shown with an explanation. Check that shares sum to v(full coalition).
- Gaming note (stated, not solved): bid windows are self-reported; a company could misstate them. Production would fix baselines from declared-in-advance or historical profiles.

### 6. Rolling re-plan (replay of one real day)

- Re-plan at 4-6 points in the day as Chalk forecasts update. Hours already executed are frozen; only remaining blocks are re-planned. Settlement uses the executed schedule.

### 7. Demo UI

- Hero: adoption chart (strategies 1-4).
- Replay panel: the day's schedule changing at each re-plan, with the per-hour marginal resource.
- Operator/auditor view: receipts for all bidders (labeled; companies would see only their own).
- Stretch C: live bid form and leaderboard.

- Strategy 4 vs 3 result reported honestly. If coordination does not beat independent-marginal, the story changes; do not tune to force it.

- Any number on screen traces to a data source or a line of code.
